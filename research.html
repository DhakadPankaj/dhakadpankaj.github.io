Research Focus

Evolution of immune genes across Drosophilidae

My work examines how immune gene repertoires diversify across lineages and how selective pressures shape coding sequence evolution and expression patterns.

Core Questions

  • Which immune genes show lineage-specific gain and loss, and adaptive evolution?
  • What predicts protein evolution rates in innate immune systems?
  • How do immune genes expression patterns vary in distant drosophilids?
  • Can transcriptomics reveal novel antimicrobial peptide candidates?
  • How do novel immune cell types emerge and evolve?

Methodologies

  • Comparative genome annotation and orthology inference
  • Gene turnover and selection analyses
  • Codon usage bias analysis
  • Comparative transcriptomics and metagenomics
  • Single cell RNA-seq analysis
  • Reproducible analysis workflows with R and Python

Research Background

During my PhD at the University of Edinburgh, I focused on comparative genomics and the evolution of immune genes across 301 species of Drosophilidae. This work identified distinctive evolutionary signatures among immune pathways and highlighted novel AMP candidates in non-model species.
