Comparative genomics meets evolutionary immunology.

Genome Annotations

Comparative protein-coding gene annotations for 301 drosophilid species using the Comparative Annotation Toolkit combined with de novo prediction. The resulting dataset provides standardized gene models and orthology assignments intended to facilitate comparative genomic studies across the family, and I used it to investigate broad patterns of genome evolution, including gene content, codon usage, and amino acid composition across Drosophilidae. Read more.

Immune Gene Evolution

Using comparative analyses of sequence evolution, positive selection, and gene family turnover, I tested how gene level properties such as expression, length, protein interactions, and protein structure influence rates of immune gene evolution. After accounting for these, I found that immune genes evolve faster at the protein sequence level than non-immune genes but are less prone to gene duplication and loss. Different immune gene classes follow distinct evolutionary trajectories, revealing how functional specialization and molecular constraints jointly shape immune diversification. Read more.
